## Best Design Patterns Found

### Hero Design Patterns

- Full-bleed image heroes work best when the product or brand signal appears immediately and the next section is hinted below the fold.
- Premium nature sites use restrained overlays, not heavy filters, so the animal or landscape still carries the emotion.
- Strong heroes usually need one primary CTA and one quieter secondary CTA. Too many choices reduce momentum.

### Navigation Patterns

- Sticky headers should be short, translucent, and conversion-oriented.
- Mobile navigation should prioritize brand recognition plus the main follow action.
- Section links are useful on desktop, but mobile users need a clean top bar more than a crowded menu.

### CTA Placement Patterns

- The first CTA belongs in the hero.
- A repeated CTA after featured content catches visitors once they understand the brand.
- Community CTAs work best when the copy explains belonging, not mechanics.

### Image and Gallery Patterns

- 9:16 cards are the right format for Facebook Reel-style browsing.
- Image crops should protect faces, eyes, and action points before decorative composition.
- Galleries feel premium when curated tightly. Too many images make a one-page site feel like a storage folder.

### Mobile Layout Patterns

- Single-column content with large tap targets is more important than desktop symmetry.
- Cards need stable aspect ratios so loading and hover states do not shift the layout.
- Hero copy should sit over a strong dark region of the image on small screens.

### Color and Font Patterns

- Dark forest black, cream type, muted warm body copy, and amber CTAs fit cinematic wildlife without feeling like a generic black-and-gold template.
- Serif headings create editorial emotion; system sans-serif body type keeps the site fast and readable.
- A restrained secondary color, such as moss green, helps avoid a one-note gold palette.

### Trust-Building Patterns

- Credibility comes from clear positioning, direct contact links, careful wording, and consistent visual quality.
- Avoiding unsupported claims is part of the brand trust strategy.
- A named community, Wild Crew, makes the page feel more established than a generic follow prompt.

### Footer, Privacy, and Contact Patterns

- Static sites should not imply forms, accounts, or tracking when none exists.
- Footer privacy notes should be plain and short.
- AI-assisted production wording belongs only in the footer/privacy area, where it is honest but not the public headline.
